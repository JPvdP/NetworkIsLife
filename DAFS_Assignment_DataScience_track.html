Data Analytics for Sustainability – data science track assignment (in pairs)

Overview of the assignment

Introduction

In the data science track of Data Analytics for Sustainability we have practiced with the collection, import, analysis and visualisation of geographic and textual data. In this assignment we will apply these skills to analyse and visualize patterns on any topic related to sustainability. The central idea is that you investigate the (spatial) relationship between two variables. The relationship can be analysed in a specific region, or you can compare two regions (e.g. Amsterdam and Utrecht) or compare scales (e.g. province vs. country) or time periods. The aim is to explore your chosen relatioship in a data driven way, combining geospatial analysis with Natural Language Processing.

What should the report contain?

  • Text (max. 4000 words). The text should contain the following sections: introduction, data and methods, results, discussion, conclusion.
  • Code. Clean and annotated code. Make sure to include only the code that was used to actually do the processing, analysis, and visualizations. Just like any other report, you don’t need to include everything you tried, but rather be transparent about the steps you took to generate your results. In addition, make sure to annotate the code using hashtags (#) to explain what you did.
  • Visualizations. Strive for a maximum of 4 visualizations. Depending on the scope of your project, these visualizations could be maps, graphs, etc.

What should be handed in?

You will hand in a .zip file containing three things:

  • The .html file generated from your Quarto document containing explanatory text and code.
  • A folder that is automatically generated when rendering your .html file (i.e. the associated files of your .html file, NOT the input data)
  • The Quarto file itself (.qmd)

You can generate a .html file from your Quarto document by clicking the ‘Render’ Button. At the beginning of your Quarto document, you should specify: format: html echo: true These settings ensure that the generated .html file shows the code you wrote to generate your visualizations. Make sure to try this out (Render), and don’t wait with this until the last day before the deadline.

Additional Requirements

Use at least two different datasets with at least one textual scope and one geograpical scope.

Textual analysis: + A topic model for each of the data sources. With a description of the process that led you to the final topics (steps in data cleaning, and method for the identification of the number of topics) + Either: (1) A Named Entity Recognition section in which you extract different elements from news articles. You will connect the elements identified to word, topics or other variables in the database in a coherent manner. Or (2) a Sentiment analysis on news articles. The sentiment scores can be connected to the other variables identified in both the NER exercise and the topic modelling exercise.

Geospatial analysis: + Combine at least two different geographic data types (raster/vector) and/or at least two different geometry types (points, lines, polygons). For example: analyse points in polygons, extract and summarize raster values within polygons, calculate distance between points and lines, etc. + Calculate an index to summarize and/or visualize the spatial relationship between variables of interest (e.g. density of points within polygons, change of value with distance, etc.)

Types of analysis

Below, we present some possible types of analysis and data sources that you may use in your assignment (obviously you will combine geospatial analyses with NLP). You are free to come up with your own design, but be aware of the time restrictions for this assignment. Using a proposed analysis type ensures data availability and feasibility.

Geospatial analysis

In this type of analysis you will focus on a spatial relationship that inspires you. For example, you could assess the relationship between income and greenness, air pollution and roads, etc. Select data sources that are relevant to the relationship under investigation. Subset/crop the data to the area of interest. Choose a scale that’s feasible in the context of this assignment (larger datasets take more time to process). Depending on the input data and type of analysis, this could be a city, province, or country (or a comparison between them). Analyse the spatial relationship between different variables. The relationship can be analysed in a specific region, or you can compare two regions (e.g. Amsterdam and Utrecht) or compare scales (e.g. province vs. country) or time periods. Create a map (or multiple maps) to visualize your results and interpret the spatial patterns. Some data sources to check out: Global:

  • NaturalEarth
  • Open Street Map
  • Administrative areas (countries, districts, etc.)
  • Biodiversity - GBIF
  • Hydrography/rivers
  • River barriers
  • Historical climate
  • Future climate
  • Anthromes
  • Human development index and GDP (gridded)
  • Geomorphology (90m)
  • Geomorphology (250m)
  • Crop suitability
  • Ecoregions
  • Languages

Countries: Many countries have a (geo)data portal. For the Netherlands there is a lot of data available on PDOK.

Cities and provinces. Many cities have a (geo)data portal. Examples for Utrecht:

  • Utrecht jive contains data for the city of Utrecht on many different subjects, much of the data is spatial (or can be joined to polygons by name)
  • Utrecht province geodata
  • Utrecht bats
  • Utrecht swifts

Technology-focused Analysis

In this type of analysis you will focus on a technology that you choose yourself. As a mandatory datasource you will use patent data. Patents are chosen because they contain a lot of valuable data you can analyse (who is doing research on this technology, where are they from, who are they collaborating with, compare companies or regions, quality assessment etc.).

To complement this data you will at least complement the data with articles from the Lexis Uni database. The main aim of this analysis will be to study a specific technology and show how it is perceived in society.

  • Some data sources to check out:

    • European Environment agency data Hub (Data related to specific technologies)
    • Lexis Uni has news paper articles and can be accessed via the UU library website
    • DBnomics (various databases)
    • Eurostat
    • Lens.org (for Patents and Scientific Publications)
    • CORDIS (european research project databases)
    • On demand we have a dataset with all dutch patents, geolocated at the NUTS3 & NUTS 4 level.

Bibliographic analysis

This type of analysis allows you to tackle questions that are less technology oriented. Think about questions related to social justice, emotions and climate transition, governance issues,… The idea is to perform a bibliographic analysis on a topic of your choice and connect this with a societal view with the help of articles from lexis uni.

You can identify who is doing research on this topic, which institutions they work for, what the main topics are, how they evolve over time. This analysis is then connected to an analysis from news sources so analyse the perception of society on the topic chosen.

  • Some data sources to check out:

    • Quality of government
    • Academic Freedom
    • Values Survey
    • European Environment agency data Hub (Data related to specific technologies)
    • DBnomics (various databases)
    • Eurostat
    • Lens.org (for Patents and Scientific Publications)
    • CORDIS (european research project databases)
    • Overton (via the UU library)
    • Lexis Nexis (via the UU library)
    • Geographical data sources are presented in the slides of the lectures

Your own idea

If none of the above options suit you, and you have a good idea you would like to pursue, go for it! Read the rubric for the course carefully and ensure that the data you have chosen connects to the rubric. In any case, contact me to explain your idea.

  • To avoid issues with the assignment ensure yourself of the following:
    • You can find two complementary data sets on your topic.
    • The topic relates to sustainability.
    • There are enough observations to perform an analysis on. Try to have at least 40 observations in each data set. And ensure that the text of the data is long enough.
    • Check the validity of the data you have and check that you have the rights to use the data.

There is a lot of data available on the internet, pay attention to the rights on this data and where it comes from.

    • Quality of government
    • Academic Freedom

    • European Environment agency data Hub (Data related to specific technologies)

AI

The use of Large Language Models (ChatGPT, LLama, Bard) to help with programming is allowed. The main focus is on the quality of the analysis of the results. You are not allowed to use data sets you have entirely generated yourself. You are of course allowed to compute additional data on an existing data set with real data. Analysis of the data should be your own work and not assisted by AI (i.e you cannot use AI to generate the analysis for you). If the teachers have a doubt with regards to the use of AI, they can request a discussion where students will be asked to present and discuss the results orally.

Schedule

If you keep up with this schedule you should be able to work on your data in each tutorial and extract new information from your data.
